% Abstract
\begin{abstract}
Multi-center volumetric medical imaging datasets suffer from scanner disparities, resolution heterogeneity, and site-dependent artifacts that impede deployment of machine learning models in clinical practice. We establish a standardized, leakage-free framework for 3D medical image classification that compares end-to-end 3D deep neural networks against quantitative anatomical biomarkers, and optimizes probability calibration for clinical risk stratification. We evaluate our framework on a multi-site cohort of 1,362 3D volumetric scans across 15 acquisition centers with 66 unique voxel sizes (1.47--3.90~mm$^3$). The pipeline comprises: (1) spatial standardization via RAS reorientation, 2.0~mm$^3$ isotropic resampling, and center-of-mass aligned $80^3$ bounding box cropping; (2) leakage-free validation via stratified group cross-validation by acquisition site; (3) model comparison between 3D deep ResNets (845K--1.3M parameters) and quantitative ROI biomarkers with gradient-boosted trees; (4) regularization via Mixup ($\alpha=0.2$), label smoothing ($\epsilon=0.05$), and 15-view test-time augmentation; (5) post-hoc Platt scaling for probability calibration. Naive random cross-validation inflates AUROC by $\sim$0.15 due to scanner-signature leakage. Site-aware group cross-validation reveals true generalizable performance. The calibrated 3D deep ResNet ensemble achieves OOF LogLoss = 0.2453 and AUROC = 0.9610, with largest gains on lower-resolution scans (+2.3\% AUROC). Platt scaling reduces Expected Calibration Error by 57\% (0.0142 $\to$ 0.0061) while preserving AUROC. Ablation studies quantify contributions of architecture depth, Mixup, label smoothing, ensemble diversity, TTA, and calibration. We present five actionable rules for robust multi-center 3D medical image classification: (1) spatial uniformity via isotropic resampling, (2) leakage audit via site-aware group validation, (3) dimensional economy via anatomical center-of-mass cropping, (4) 3D regularization via Mixup and TTA, (5) calibration-first deployment via Platt scaling.
\end{abstract}
