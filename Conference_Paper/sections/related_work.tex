\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\noindent\textbf{DaT-SPECT \& Deep Learning for Parkinson's:} Quantitative Striatal Binding Ratio (SBR) analysis has long served as the clinical standard for quantifying dopaminergic deficit in $^{123}\text{I}$-Ioflupane SPECT imaging~\cite{seibyl1998single}. Recent advances in 3D Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) demonstrate automated, end-to-end feature extraction from volumetric neuroimages without manual Region-of-Interest (ROI) segmentation~\cite{korkinof2018high, rahim2019riemannian}. However, most existing deep learning models are evaluated on single-center cohorts or homogeneous public benchmark repositories, leaving performance under real-world multi-center scanner heterogeneity largely unexplored.

\noindent\textbf{Scanner \& Site Effects in Multi-Center Cohorts:} Cross-hospital image acquisition introduces domain shifts caused by variations in scanner vendor hardware, gamma camera collimators, reconstruction algorithms, and voxel spatial resolutions~\cite{glocker2019machine}. To mitigate these site effects, prior studies have explored statistical feature harmonization (e.g., ComBat~\cite{fortin2018harmonization}) and domain generalization or domain-adversarial neural training~\cite{ganin2016domain, dou2019domain, kamnitsas2017unsupervised}. While effective, these techniques often assume available site labels during inference or require complex multi-task adversarial objectives, whereas standardized spatial preprocessing offers a lightweight, deterministic alternative.

\noindent\textbf{Data Leakage \& Validation Rigor in Medical ML:} A major failure mode in multi-center medical machine learning is scanner-site data leakage~\cite{subbanna2021review}. When scans from the same hospital site or scanner model are randomly split across training and validation folds, models learn site-specific acquisition confounders rather than pathophysiological biomarkers~\cite{zech2018variable}. This issue leads to severe overestimation of diagnostic accuracy in neuroimaging and chest radiograph benchmarks~\cite{varoquaux2017cross, wen2020convolutional}. Site-aware group cross-validation is therefore mandatory to establish true clinical generalizability on held-out acquisition centers.

\noindent\textbf{Probability Calibration \& Ensemble Confidence Dynamics:} Reliable clinical decision support requires well-calibrated posterior probabilities~\cite{guo2017calibration}. While individual deep neural networks tend to exhibit overconfidence, multi-model ensembling and multi-view Test-Time Augmentation (TTA) can induce probability softening or underconfidence ($a > 1.0$) by compressing predicted probabilities towards $p=0.5$~\cite{platt1999probabilistic, saeed2022test}. Post-hoc calibration must therefore account for these ensemble confidence shifts without introducing evaluation leakage across folds.

\noindent\textbf{The Research Gap:} Prior work addresses isolated components of the deployment pipeline—such as harmonization, site validation, or probability calibration—in isolation. In contrast, our framework evaluates the complete, end-to-end processing chain (spatial standardization, site-aware group validation, ensemble TTA, and nested calibration) on a multi-center DaT-SPECT cohort.
