\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a systematic development cycle for robust 3D medical image classification across heterogeneous scanners, demonstrated on a 1,362-scan DaT-SPECT cohort across 15 acquisition centers. Through a four-stage framework -- spatial standardization, dual-track modeling, leakage-free validation, and probability calibration -- we established a new benchmark for multi-center 3D classification.

Our final model (V3) employs a \textbf{10-model deep ResNet ensemble} (5 Net3dR-v25 + 5 Net3dBig-v25) with \textbf{3 residual blocks and 3 downsampling stages}, trained with \textbf{Mixup ($\alpha=0.2$), label smoothing ($\epsilon=0.05$), cosine annealing, and gradient accumulation} on \textbf{full 80$^3$ volumes at 2.0~\voxelunit~isotropic}. \textbf{15-view test-time augmentation} and \textbf{Platt calibration} further improve performance.

V3 achieves \textbf{OOF LogLoss = 0.2453 (calibrated)} and \textbf{AUROC = 0.9610}, an 18.1\% LogLoss reduction and 2.1\% AUROC gain over the strong V2 baseline. Improvements are consistent across all scanner resolution tiers, with \textbf{largest gains on lower-resolution scans (+2.3\% AUROC)}. All improvements are statistically significant ($p<0.05$).

We provide comprehensive ablation studies identifying the key drivers: architecture scaling (largest factor), Mixup+label smoothing, ensemble diversity, TTA, and calibration. Our leakage-prevention methodology (site-aware group CV, per-volume normalization, OOF-only calibration) sets a rigorous standard for multi-site medical imaging benchmarks.

We distill our findings into \textbf{five actionable rules} for medical ML practitioners: (1) Spatial Uniformity, (2) Leakage Audit, (3) Dimensional Economy, (4) 3D Regularization, (5) Calibration First. These rules provide a practical checklist for deploying 3D classifiers in multi-center clinical environments across diverse modalities (CT, MRI, PET, SPECT).

The code, weights, and visualizations are publicly available for reproducibility. This work establishes a generalized blueprint for robust multi-center 3D medical image classification, scanner-disparity mitigation, and probability calibration -- directly applicable to clinical deployment of AI in medical imaging.
